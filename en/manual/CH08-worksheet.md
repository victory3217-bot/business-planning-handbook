# CH08 Hypothesis Validation and Pivoting Design Worksheet

> **How to use:** This worksheet can be completed on a client's own new-business project. Read "I / my" as your team, business unit, or in-house venture team rather than an individual founder.

> Source: Chapter 8 (KM064–KM073). This worksheet takes the questions and items from KM066 (The Four Elements of Validation Design), KM067 (Small, Repeatable Experiments), KM068 (Data-Driven Pivoting), and KM073 (The Execute → Data → Revise → Re-execute Loop) and lists them as check points for reference, not as fill-in fields. No new methodology has been added. Section 5 is a supplementary item that applies the items above to writing the execution plan.

---

## 1. The Four Elements of Validation Design (KM066)

Take the questions that arose while reviewing the earlier chapters (customer, value, channel, price, effective market, etc.) and break them down into the four items below.

> Check point: ① What to Investigate — Decide what you want to check right now. (e.g., "Are there no overseas customers?" "Does the need for localization actually exist?")

> Check point: ② Metrics — Decide what specifically you will measure.

> Check point: ③ Method and Plan — Decide what method you will use (Interview / VOC / Key-man research / Desk research / Other).

> Check point: ④ Feedback Data — Link this validation result to the component (customer, value, channel, price, market boundary, etc.) it will serve as grounds for revising.

> Check: Confirm that this research has been connected in advance to "which hypothesis it's for, who you'll meet, what answer or behavior you'll confirm, and which decision the result will change."

---

## 2. Designing a Small, Repeatable Experiment (KM067)

> Check point: Find the methods you can start with, without a finished product (Interview / VOC / Key-man research / Partner outreach, etc.).

> Check point: Check that the cost and time going into this experiment are at an affordable level. Do not bet the business's fate on the MVP.

> Check point: Find a way to shrink this experiment to be smaller than the business's full scale. (e.g., testing one channel before nationwide sales, a sample store before full rollout, confirming customer behavior before building all the features)

> Check point: Distinguish whether this experiment will make the next decision more accurate or is meant to show off its chances of success.

---

## 3. Data-Driven Pivoting Judgment (KM068)

> Check point: Distinguish whether, as a result of validation, the data supported the existing hypothesis, prompted a revision, or led you to discard it.

> Check point: If you are considering a pivot, check that the basis for the judgment is "the data shook the existing hypothesis," rather than "a new idea came up."

> Check point: Identify which assumption turned out to be wrong (customer / value / channel / price / market boundary) and check the evidence for it.

---

## 4. The Five Questions of the Execute → Data → Revise → Re-execute Loop (KM073)

> Check point: Before Validation — Decide what the riskiest or most uncertain hypothesis is right now, and what needs to be observed or measured.

> Check point: Design the Execution — Decide what the smallest affordable action is that can confirm this hypothesis.

> Check point: After Validation — Distinguish whether the data supported the existing hypothesis, prompted a revision, or led you to discard it.

> Check point: Pivoting — Decide which note (component) to reopen based on the result, and what needs to change.

> Check point: Knowledge Accumulation — Decide where to store the back data and execution results used in this judgment so they can be reused in the next decision.

---

## 5. Execution Plan with Check Metrics (KM066, KM067, KM069)

> Check point: A. Roadmap — Pair what you will do in each period with the metric you will check at that point. (Period / What to do / Check metric)

> Check point: Decide in advance when, and by what, you would notice that this plan is wrong.

> Check point: B. Three risks and responses — Do not write "no risks." For each risk, pair the condition that counts as triggered with the pre-decided response action. (Risk / Trigger condition / Response action)

> Check point: C. Decide the criteria before seeing the results. Decide in advance what results would count as supporting the hypothesis, revising it, or discarding it.

> Check point: D. Marking evidence status — Mark each major number and statement in the plan as fact, assumption, or to be verified, and decide how and when to verify it. (Number / statement; Evidence status; How and when to verify)

> Check point: E. Progress status — If there is little already done that you can write in the business plan or application, sort what you have into done, in progress, and planned.

> Check point: Decide who the target customers are whose reactions you can collect, or what prototype you can build, before submission.
