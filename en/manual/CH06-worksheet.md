> **How to use:** This worksheet can be completed on a client's own new-business project. Read "I / my" as your team, business unit, or in-house venture team rather than an individual founder.

## 2. Value-Based Pricing (KM046)

> Note: Check what problem the customer solves, or what value they feel they gain, from our product/service. Price starts from this value, not from cost.

- What price range and willingness to pay will customers accept? (results of customer research/market response testing)

> Note: Check how we can adjust cost and product composition so that the business is viable at that price. Set the price the customer accepts first, then fit cost and product composition to it.

> Note: Check what basis of value (product, channel, service, brand) we must design and prove to justify a higher price. This basis is the standard that justifies the higher price.

## 3. Competition-Based Pricing and Positioning (KM047)

- Who are the actual competitors (including substitutes) that offer similar customer value to the same customer segment as ours?
- What are those competitors' price levels?
- Are we choosing a low-price strategy or a high-price strategy? Are the conditions required by that choice in place (low price: accepting a lower operating margin and capital risk / high price: product, service, customer experience, channel, brand)?
- What is our answer to "what customer, and what value, do we want to be remembered for?" rather than "how much should we charge?"

## 4. Channel and Price Structure (KM048)

- Is the channel we use direct, indirect, or a mix?
- If we use an indirect channel, what are the fees and distribution margin, and are they reflected in the price structure?
- If we use a direct channel, do we have the resources to handle customer acquisition, shipping, and service ourselves?
- Do we need a price band between the consumer price and the actual sale price for discounts, commissions, or consignment sales fees? If so, how large should that band be?

> Check point: Check whether the fee rate is based on the selling price or on the amount actually paid out, and whether the base is the same across channels.

> Check point: Check whether the channel with the largest revenue is the same as the channel with the largest contribution margin per order. If not, see whether the larger-revenue channel plays another role, such as acquiring new customers.

> Check point: Check whether the actual product/service direct cost is higher than the allowable cost calculated backward from the positioning price and target profit. If so, see what to change (channel, price, product composition, target profit).

## 5. Customer Relationship Cost (KM049)

> Check point: Check how much it costs to acquire customers (personnel, systems, promotion).

> Check point: Check how much it costs to manage the purchase experience and drive repeat/additional purchases.

> Check point: Check whether this cost is reflected in the current price structure (cost + margin).

## 6. Revenue Streams and Revenue Model (KM050)

> Check point: Check who pays, what they pay for, and how much they pay.

Answer the following questions.

- When does the money come in?
- Do they pay once, or repeatedly?

## 7. One-Time Revenue vs. Recurring Revenue (KM051)

- Is our business a model where the relationship ends after selling the product once, or a model where continuous relationship management after purchase generates recurring revenue?
- If we carry fixed costs, must we acquire a new customer every time, or do existing customers generate revenue again?
- Beyond the initial purchase price, have we considered the cost required for the customer relationship together with the possibility of recurring revenue?

## 8. Pricing Mechanism (KM052)

Mark how our business charges its price: □ Fixed price □ Negotiated price □ Volume-based unit price (B2B) □ Bidding/procurement conditions (B2G) □ Other ( )

- Which of the following is this method most closely tied to: the negotiating power of the parties, inventory, timing, supply and demand, or bidding conditions?

## 9. Three Financial Lenses (KM053)

> Check point: P&L — Check how much margin remains, given price, sales volume, cost, and SG&A.

> Check point: Asset — Check what asset (value) accumulates through business activity.

> Check point: Cash Flow — Check when cash comes in and when it goes out, and whether there is any point where cash pressure could arise.

## 10. Checkpoint Before Moving to Business Feasibility (KM054)

Confirm that the following three items have been organized.

- [ ] The price (P) grounded in customer and value has been organized.
- [ ] The cost and margin structure that lets the business sustain itself at that price has been organized.
- [ ] The revenue model explaining what the customer pays for and how has been organized.

If any of these three items is not yet organized, return to items 1–9 above and check again before moving on to calculating market size (P×Qty).
