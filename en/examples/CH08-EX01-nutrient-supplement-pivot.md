# CH08-EX01. Nutrient Supplement Field Validation Pivot Case

- **Related Chapter:** CH08 Execute, Validate, and Revise Through Pivoting
- **Related MN:** MN08 Execution, Validation, Pivoting, and Business Planning
- **Related KM:** KM068 Data-Driven Pivoting

## What This Case Illustrates

This case shows how the hypothesis that "people will seek out an old nutrient supplement again out of nostalgia" fell apart in the course of actual sales activity. The business team assumed customers would come back to the product because of nostalgia for the past, but in actual field validation, customers cared more about efficacy than nostalgia. Based on this data, the team had to revise its messaging and its approach to customers.

This case demonstrates the principle that pivoting does not mean "abandoning your original idea" — it means "a result of new data revising an existing hypothesis." In other words, the basis for pivoting is not whether a new idea came up, but whether actual data shook the existing hypothesis (in this case, the purchase-motivation hypothesis of "nostalgia").

## Reference

The same KM also presents a gallery case alongside this one. In that case, the team defined its current revenue source and the shape of business it wanted, carried out a small execution, and — once market reaction appeared — readjusted the relationship between its target customer and its key resources. Together, the two cases show that "even a business model that once worked well can change again as the market moves," and that "pivoting is not a one-time event but a normal learning mechanism within the business-development process."
