# CH06-EX01. Cases Where Channel Structure Changes Price and Margin

- Related Chapter: CH06 Designing Cost, Price, and Revenue Model
- Related MN: MN06 Cost, Price, and Revenue Model
- Related KM: KM048-channel-pricing-economics.md

## What This Case Illustrates

These are three cases showing how, even under the same principle that "price does not move alone," different channels — B2C direct/D2C, B2B bulk orders, and B2G public procurement — lead to different price and margin structures. All three cases show that price is a strategic variable continually revised by channel, volume, and transaction structure.

## Case 1. B2C — Vegan Bean-to-Bar Chocolate: Sustaining a High Cost Structure Through D2C

Manufacturing cost was about 56% of the actual sale price, and SG&A was around 15% — a high cost structure. To secure an adequate margin, the company chose direct-to-consumer (D2C) online sales as its core channel, reducing distribution stages, and used part of the gap between the consumer price and the actual sale price for discounts, free gifts, and other customer relationship management.

## Case 2. B2B — Cosmetics Containers: Volume Changes Price

The official supply price differed from the actual sale price based on large-volume orders. The company designed pricing so that the unit price fell while still maintaining a margin of about 25% once an order of around 200,000 units was secured, with economies of scale from mass production as the key premise. It cut intermediate distribution stages and pitched directly to cosmetics manufacturers to lower fees.

## Case 3. B2G — Groundwater Level Measuring Equipment: Public Procurement's Transaction Structure Enters the Price

The company set a price band between the consumer price and the actual sale price. This was to account for the discounts, commissions, consignment sales fees, and the possibility of future supply-price negotiations with distribution partners that arise in the public procurement process. At the same time, it set direct proposal sales to local governments as its basic channel.

## Reading the Numbers: Aligning to an Actual Sale Price of 100

When the price diagrams of the three cases are aligned to the same base (actual sale price = 100), it is easy to see at a glance how the weight of cost and margin differs by transaction relationship. The ratios were calculated from the values below.

| Case | Actual Sale Price | Total Cost | Margin | Total Cost Ratio | Margin Rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B2C bean-to-bar chocolate | 15,900 won | 11,385 won (manufacturing cost 9,000 + SG&A 2,385) | 4,515 won | 71.6% | 28.4% |
| B2B cosmetics containers | 1,250 won | 944 won | 306 won | 75.5% | 24.5% |
| B2G groundwater level measuring equipment | 1.1 million won | 670,000 won (manufacturing cost 340,000 + SG&A 330,000) | 430,000 won | 60.9% | 39.1% |

- The B2C composition ratios are manufacturing cost 56.6%, SG&A 15.0%, and margin 28.4%, differing from the 56%, 15%, and 28% in Case 1 above only by rounding.
- In B2B, the 250 won (20% of the actual sale price) between the official supply price of 1,500 won and the actual sale price of 1,250 won based on large-volume orders is the band used for volume discounts and relationship management.
- In B2G, the SG&A of 330,000 won corresponds to 30% of the actual sale price, and this band can be set aside for commissions and dealer consignment sales fees.

### Two places where the figures look inconsistent use the values confirmed by calculation

- **The B2G margin is 430,000 won.** In the source material there is a place where the margin is written as 330,000 won, but 110 - (34 + 33) = 43 (in units of 10,000 won), and the ratio is 39.1%. The 33 is the SG&A figure, not the margin, and 43 is the value that is consistent with both an SG&A ratio of 30% and a margin rate of about 40%.
- **The B2C consumer price of 19,000 won is read as a rounded list price.** Adding a price band of about 20% to the actual sale price of 15,900 won gives 19,080 won. The 80 won difference from the stated consumer price of 19,000 won is understood to come from rounding the last digits when the price was set, and the band is actually 3,100 won (19.5%). Use 19,000 won for calculations based on the consumer price and 15,900 won for structure calculations, and state which one you are using.

These figures are case values taken from the price diagrams at the time the cases were written, and the source material does not give the year or market conditions of the cases. Do not read them as current prices or industry averages.

## Common Implication

What the three cases have in common is that price keeps being revised by other elements of the business model (customer, channel, production capacity, order volume, relationship cost, partners). It is appropriate to view price not as a fixed number but as a strategic variable to be examined as the business structure changes.
