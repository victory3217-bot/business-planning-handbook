# CH07-EX01. Bean-to-Bar Chocolate Case — How Qty Is Derived in a B2C Effective Market

- Related Chapter: CH07 (Validating Business Feasibility and the Effective Market)
- Related MN: MN07 Business Feasibility, Effective Market, and Projected Financials
- Related KM: KM062-p-times-qty-by-transaction-type.md

## What This Case Illustrates

This case shows what data is used to explain the basis for quantity sold (Qty) in a B2C transaction, when expressing Effective Market size as P (price) × Qty (quantity sold). It demonstrates, with a real case, the principle that when the individual consumer is the buyer, "at what price, how many people, and how often will they buy" becomes the core logic explaining Qty.

## Case Details

In the bean-to-bar chocolate case, in order to absorb a high manufacturing cost, the revenue structure was designed around an online D2C channel, and the core target was narrowed to premium vegan customers. The Effective Market size was calculated by combining the product price, the size of the target customer base, and the annual consumption volume. The case combined three elements — a base-unit price of 19,000 won, an approximate vegan and potential-vegetarian population of 1.5 million, and an annual consumption volume — to derive the annual Effective Market size.

This figure is best understood not as the latest current market statistic, but as a case value illustrating what data the framework uses as Qty in a B2C context.

## Reading the Numbers: Which Price Was Multiplied

The case values are a base-unit (60g) price of 19,000 won, a vegan and potential-vegetarian population of about 1.5 million (population counted in persons), and an annual consumption of 600g (10 base units). Multiplying them gives the following.

| Calculation | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Annual spending per person | 19,000 won × 10 units | 190,000 won |
| Effective Market at consumer price | 19,000 won × 1,500,000 persons × 10 units | 285.0 billion won |
| Size at actual sale price | 15,900 won × 1,500,000 persons × 10 units | 238.5 billion won |

The difference of 46.5 billion won between the two results is the share corresponding to the price band (3,100 won) between the consumer price and the actual sale price. In this case, that band was used for customer relationship management such as discounts and free gifts (see CH06-EX01).

The 285.0 billion won in this case is the value multiplied at the consumer price. This fits the buyer-defined market definition in CH07, in which the Effective Market is the amount buyers spend. When speaking of the size the business can actually book as revenue, use the 238.5 billion won multiplied at the actual sale price. Either way, state the basis within the document and do not mix them.

### Changing One Assumption at a Time

This is a linear calculation that changes only one assumption from the original value (285.0 billion won) and leaves the rest as they are. It does not include the effect of demand changing when the price is lowered.

| Scenario | Price | Target population | Annual purchases | Effective Market | vs. original |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original | 19,000 won | 1.5 million | 10 units | 285.0 billion won | 100% |
| Price cut 10% | 17,100 won | 1.5 million | 10 units | 256.5 billion won | 90% |
| Target down 20% | 19,000 won | 1.2 million | 10 units | 228.0 billion won | 80% |
| Annual purchases halved | 19,000 won | 1.5 million | 5 units | 142.5 billion won | 50% |

Because the three assumptions are linked by multiplication, the result is directly proportional to each rate of change. In this case, the assumption that swings the most is purchase frequency. This means the assumption that the entire vegan and potential-vegetarian population buys 10 units a year is the first one to verify.

## Implications of This Case

In calculating a B2C Effective Market, Qty only becomes an explainable number when you combine a narrowed target population that actually fits the product concept (premium vegan chocolate) — rather than the entire consumer population — with price and purchase frequency (annual consumption volume). This also shows that the choice to narrow the target itself is connected to earlier business-model decisions about channel (D2C) and price (19,000 won).
