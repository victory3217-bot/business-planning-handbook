# Appendix C. A Framework for Channel Decisions

> This appendix is supplementary material drawn from the standard lecture materials that organize the author's teaching and consulting results, not from the handbook body (which is Knowledge-Module based). 7C and the funnel are the author's own frameworks.

## Questions This Chapter Answers

- When sales are weak, how do you find the one stage where things are blocked, rather than doubting the channel as a whole?
- With which seven questions do you ask why it is blocked?
- What changes when you divide channels by customer behavior instead of by name?
- Why is the criterion for choosing a channel contribution margin per order rather than revenue?
- What sentence do you leave behind after running one full loop from observation to execution?

## 1. Core Concepts

CH05 placed the channel as one box of the BMC and checked whether it fits the customer and the value proposition, and CH06 covered how channel fees and distribution margin change the price structure. This appendix organizes the next question: the order of judgment for deciding where to concentrate and what to give up among several channel candidates.

The judgment takes four steps. Use the funnel to find **where it is blocked**, use 7C to ask **why it is blocked**, compare **what money remains** through contribution margin per order, and write the result as a one-sentence **decision**. The purchase journey sits in front of these. The journey is the order in which the customer moves; the funnel is how that movement shows up as numbers in a channel. It is less confusing to refer to the two by name than by number (editorial suggestion).

There are two core principles. A sales slump is not a problem of the whole but a problem of a specific stage, so look for the bottleneck in one place first. And once you have set up candidate causes, do not prescribe by gut feeling; confirm them with numbers.

## 2. Key Frameworks and Mental Models

### A channel is a chain of businesses

A channel is the chain of businesses that a product or service passes through until it reaches the final buyer. It is a two-way structure: goods flow from producer to consumer and payment flows from consumer to producer, and the more intermediaries there are, the longer the chain and the higher the price can become. Direct and indirect channels are sometimes used together. The chain does four things: passing information, transferring ownership, logistics, and collecting payment. The buyer who pays at the end of the chain and the consumer or user who actually uses the product may not be the same person. Gifts and corporate purchases are such cases.

The route from producer to buyer can be divided into five: direct sales, via retail, via wholesale and retail, via an agent and retail, and via an agent, wholesale, and retail. The longer the route, the wider the reach, but margin and control change.

The channel carries eight flows: physical possession, ownership, promotion, negotiation, financing, risk, ordering, and payment. Which business carries which flow is the channel structure itself, and each flow comes with a cost (storage and delivery, holding inventory, promotion and publicity, time and legal work, credit and sales terms, damage and spoilage, order processing, and payment collection and bad debt). Distribution intensity divides into intensive distribution, with many businesses; selective distribution, with few; and exclusive distribution, with only one.

Channel conflict arises when the behavior of one channel member blocks the channel's goals. It is divided into four kinds: vertical, horizontal, between types, and multichannel, and its results can include price wars, customer complaints, weaker sales, defection of distributors, and reputation damage. Channel power is the ability of one member to change the behavior of another, and divides into five kinds: coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, and referent power.

### The evolution of distribution and multichannel

Distribution is organized into four stages: single channel, multichannel, omnichannel, and unified commerce. What the customer feels at each is "I have to go there to buy," "they sell in many places but each is different," "it connects wherever I buy," and "the brand recognizes me." Unified commerce is the state in which customers, inventory, and orders move as a single set of data. Phygital, which combines online and offline, is explained through three connections: customer connection (online and offline purchase history accumulate under one ID), inventory connection (store inventory is sold online in real time), and experience connection (no break from browsing through trying to payment). If any one is cut, it remains multichannel rather than phygital.

The heart of multichannel is not the number of channels you list on but how differently you have designed the reason to buy for each channel. Channel strategy is not about listing in many places but about redesigning the product name, composition, price, packaging, content, and reason to buy for each channel, and distribution strategy is the activity of deciding where to concentrate limited resources and what to give up. The purchase situation can itself decide the channel. A case that defined the relationship-and-gift situation, where buyer and user are separate, as the channel itself is an example. So when choosing a channel, look not only at the product but also at the situation in which the purchase happens.

### The purchase journey: ask where the customer is

The journey has seven stages: Discovery → Interest → Comparison → Purchase → Receipt & use → Repurchase → Referral. At each stage, one question checks the customer's position.

For example, the question at Discovery is "Where does the customer first encounter the product?", at Comparison "What does the customer compare among price, reviews, brand, and delivery?", and at Repurchase "What reason does the customer have to buy again?" A stage whose answer is blank is the first one to investigate.

### The funnel: find where it is blocked

| Funnel stage | Key question |
|---|---|
| Demand research | Is there real demand? |
| Product & price | Does this composition at this price leave a profit? |
| Exposure | Does it appear in front of customers often enough? |
| Click | Why does the customer click on the product? |
| Conversion | Why does the customer buy or drop off? |
| Amplification | How do we expand sales repeatedly? |
| Improvement | What will we measure and improve? |

Each stage has data to look at (search volume, cost and fees, search ranking, click-through rate, conversion rate, repurchase data, P&L). A stage without data cannot be diagnosed for bottlenecks, so request the data first. The links from symptom to candidate cause are as follows.

If there is plenty of exposure but no clicks, first suspect the product name, thumbnail, and price perception; if there are many clicks but no purchases, suspect the detail page, reviews, and delivery terms; if there is no repurchase, suspect product satisfaction or the absence of a customer relationship. Then confirm with the relevant numbers (click-through rate, conversion rate, review rate, and so on).

### Representative KPIs for each funnel stage

The numbers typically watched at each funnel stage are as follows.

| Funnel stage | Representative KPIs |
|---|---|
| Demand research | Search volume, category growth rate |
| Product & price | Price acceptance, gross margin |
| Exposure | Reach, impressions, search ranking |
| Click | CTR, PDP inflow rate |
| Conversion | CVR, cart abandonment rate |
| Amplification | Review rate, share rate, referral conversion rate |
| Improvement | Improvement cycle, CAC improvement rate, LTV |

The ratios between stages are calculated as click-through rate = clicks ÷ impressions, conversion rate = purchases ÷ clicks, and repurchase rate = repeat buyers ÷ purchases.

### 7C: ask why it is blocked

If the funnel finds where it is blocked, 7C finds why. It is a common language for comparing different channels with the same seven questions. A mnemonic: customer, product, role, conversion, cost, data, operating capability.

| 7C lens | Key question |
|---|---|
| Customer & Context | Who buys, when, and why? |
| Category & Product | In what way should this product be sold? |
| Channel Role | Is this channel responsible for discovery, comparison, purchase, or repurchase? |
| Conversion Driver | Which of price, reviews, content, and benefits creates the purchase? |
| Cost & Cash | How much actually remains, and when does the cash come in? |
| Control & Data | Who holds the customer information and operating data? |
| Capability & Continuity | Do we have the capability to keep operating this channel? |

Once the bottleneck is set, the first box to look at is set.

| Bottleneck | 7C lens to look at first |
|---|---|
| Insufficient demand | Customer & Context |
| Weak product or price | Category & Product |
| Insufficient exposure | Channel Role |
| Insufficient clicks | Conversion Driver |
| Insufficient conversion | Conversion Driver and Category & Product |
| Insufficient repurchase | Control & Data (together with Cost & Cash) |
| Worsening profitability | Cost & Cash |

If the first box does not explain it, widen to the second. For example, for insufficient exposure, look at Channel Role and then Capability & Continuity; for insufficient clicks, Conversion Driver and then Customer & Context; for insufficient conversion, Conversion Driver and then Category & Product.

### Channel types: divide by customer behavior, not by name

Even the same channel changes its role depending on what the customer does there, and each type has different numbers to watch (KPIs). So you must set the type first in order to read the numbers.

| Type | Customer behavior |
|---|---|
| Search-type | Searches by keyword for a product that solves a problem |
| Instant-purchase | Pays and orders right away |
| Discovery-type | Forms interest while browsing |
| Comparison-and-selection | Decides after comparing reviews, prices, and options |
| Relationship-and-gift | Recommends, shares, and gives gifts |
| Experience-type | Offline testing, demonstrations, and consultations |
| Relationship-maintenance | Manages points, repurchase, and benefits |

One channel can span two types. A broadcast-style channel such as live commerce is treated as a channel, not as a separate type, and the type is decided with supporting evidence (instructor's synthesis). Rather than ranking channels against each other, strategy is assigning each a role that fits customer behavior and product characteristics, and when a role is assigned wrongly, the channel goes astray.

### The numbers to watch by type

The representative touchpoints where customers meet each type, and its key KPIs, are as follows.

| Type | Representative touchpoints | Key KPIs |
|---|---|---|
| Search-type | Search ads, portal search, marketplace search | CTR, search conversion rate, CAC |
| Instant-purchase | Own online store, marketplace deals, app-push landing | Conversion rate, average order value, cart abandonment rate |
| Discovery-type | Short-form video, SNS feeds, recommendation areas, displays | Reach rate, save rate, PDP inflow rate |
| Comparison-and-selection | Reviews, detail pages, comparison tables, consultations | PDP conversion rate, review viewing rate |
| Relationship-and-gift | Gifting, referral links, sharing, bundles | Gift purchase rate, referral conversion rate |
| Experience-type | Stores, pop-ups, sampling, fittings | Post-experience purchase rate, dwell time, membership conversion rate |
| Relationship-maintenance | Membership apps, CRM, messengers, email | Repurchase rate, dormant-return rate, LTV |

The definitions and standards of KPIs differ by company. The table above is a frame for reading types, and the type of a particular channel is set with supporting evidence.

### A framework for comparing contribution margin per order by channel

The criterion for choosing a channel is not revenue but contribution margin per order (the actual sale price minus all variable costs) and its ratio. Only the line items are given and the numbers are left blank. Fill in the values after checking each channel's conditions.

| Item | Channel A | Channel B | Channel C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Actual sale price (after discounts, before coupon deduction) | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| − Product cost | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| − Fees and advertising | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| − Packaging, shipping, and return reserve | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| − Coupons and variable operating costs | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| = Contribution margin per order | ○ | ○ | ○ |
| Contribution margin ratio (contribution margin per order ÷ actual sale price) | ○ | ○ | ○ |

Deduct the coupon only once, as a line item, and look at fees and the return reserve on the actual sale price before the coupon deduction. Multiplying by the number of monthly purchases shows that the revenue ranking and the contribution margin ranking can differ. A channel that loses money the more it sells is a leak, not growth. The calculation of break-even, target price, and allowable cost is continued in the *Price Is Strategy* handbook.

### OODA: run one loop and leave something behind

Observe gathers confirmed facts into a fact sheet; Orient interprets the bottleneck and its cause into a funnel-and-7C diagnosis table; Decide chooses the main channel, supporting channels, and channels to give up into a channel portfolio; and Act sets the execution tasks and KPIs into an execution plan. Each stage leaves a deliverable.

### Compare candidate channels on eight items

When comparing candidate channels, use eight items: role, main customer, conversion driver, strengths, weaknesses, data acquisition ability, operational difficulty, and strategic fit. Data acquisition ability, operational difficulty, and strategic fit are marked high, medium, or low. This comparison is not a ranking of channels but a comparison of role and fit.

After comparing on the eight items, write the decision by these rules.

- Give three channels one role each: main channel, supporting channel, and channel to give up.
- The conclusion "strengthen every channel" is invalid.
- The reason for the choice must include at least two of customer, bottleneck, 7C, and profit.
- A channel to give up does not mean never doing it; it means not concentrating on it for now.
- The final check is whether it would still be the same channel if the budget were halved.
- Execution tasks must have the task, KPI, and person in charge on one line for each 30-, 60-, and 90-day period.

### Channel control is not defined as a single framework

Nowhere is "channel control" defined as a single framework, and the content related to control is spread over several places. Control & Data in 7C asks who holds customer information and operating data, and the candidate-channel comparison items include data acquisition ability. Influence among channel members is handled through the five kinds of channel power. The standard lecture materials do not explain control over data and power among members as connected to each other. So when evaluating a channel, check Control & Data, data acquisition ability, and channel power separately.

### Manage changing values together with a reference date

Values that change often, such as fees, settlement cycles, and policies, are not written directly in the text or slides but managed separately together with the value, source, and reference date. This appendix does not include such values either.

### The structure of channel judgment as read from cases

The case cards in the standard lecture materials are generalized so that they show structure rather than a specific company, and they do not state actual sales results. For each case, the example decision is divided into main channel, supporting channel, and hold, and the "evidence needed for a different answer to hold" is written alongside. Only the lessons drawn from the structure are carried over here, without numbers.

- **Special-price channel case**: A special-price channel has large sales, but you must also calculate its relationship with the regular price and other sales lines. The revenue ranking and the contribution-margin ranking can differ.
- **Regional co-brand case**: Start with the channel that can sell through the volume, not the channel with the largest profit per set.
- **B2B delivery case**: Make a large investment only after confirming the intent to buy through a small pilot.
- **Overseas channel case**: Not making overseas the main channel when there is no evidence is also a decision. Do not merge different kinds of evidence (for example, overseas agreement experience and domestic field validation) and speak of them as one.
- **Early-stage startup in an emerging market case**: Break a blocked channel down into customer relationships, key resources, and key activities to find a path for expansion.

## 3. Application Process

1. **Answer the seven journey questions.** For a box where you cannot cite evidence, do not fill it with a guess; write how you will check.
2. **Gather the funnel numbers by channel.** For a stage without data, request the data first.
3. **Pick the single weakest stage for each channel.**
4. **Set candidate causes from the symptoms** and check them against the numbers to be confirmed first.
5. **Decide which 7C box to look at first** and answer that box's question with evidence. Widen to the second box if needed.
6. **Set the channel type** and reread the numbers with that type's KPIs.
7. **Fill in the contribution-margin-per-order table by channel.** Leave boxes whose values are unconfirmed blank and mark them as assumptions.
8. **Write the decision sentence.** If you cannot fill it in without gaps, it is not yet a verdict.
   - Basic form: the main channel, the basis for choosing it (the core customer's purchase behavior and product characteristics), and the supporting channels and the roles they will be given.
   - Execution form: the period, the ratio of resources for the main and supporting channels, the purpose of the supporting channels, the KPIs and reference period used to judge stopping, and the alternative to switch to if the KPI is not met.
9. **Check whether it falls under an invalidating condition.** If any of these applies, rewrite it: the main channel is unclear, every channel is strengthened, the roles of the channels are the same, there is no basis for the selection, there are no KPIs or review criteria, or it ends with "actively use" or "operate flexibly."
10. **Feed the result back into the next observation.** Gather the execution results as new facts and run OODA again.

## 4. Common Judgment Errors

- **Treating a sales slump as a problem of the whole**: Increasing advertising or cutting the price before finding the bottleneck stage. If the bottleneck differs by channel, one prescription will not solve it.
- **Fixing a role by channel name**: Even the same channel spans different types depending on customer behavior. Reading KPIs without setting the type makes the meaning of the numbers go astray.
- **Giving the same role to several channels**: Running several channels with the same role only divides resources.
- **Reading the revenue ranking as the profit ranking**: Without calculating contribution margin per order, a channel that loses money the more it sells looks like growth.
- **A decision sentence with nothing given up**: A sentence saying every channel will be strengthened is not a decision.
- **Treating the number of channels listed on as the strategy**: What matters is not the number of channels but how differently you have designed the reason to buy for each channel.
- **Writing changing values without a reference date**: Manage fees, settlement cycles, and policies together with the value, source, and reference date.

## 5. Practical Application Questions

- Where do our customers first meet us, and which of the seven journey questions has the emptiest answer?
- Is the weakest stage the same for every channel, or different?
- Which 7C box explains the cause at that stage, and what are the supporting numbers?
- Is the revenue ranking the same as the contribution-margin-per-order ranking?
- Does the decision sentence include a channel to be given up? Would the conclusion be the same if the budget were halved?
- Is the reason to buy designed differently for each channel you have listed on?
- Have you checked channel control separately through Control & Data, data acquisition ability, and channel power?

## Key Takeaways

- Judgment proceeds in the order of where it is blocked (funnel), why it is blocked (7C), what money remains (contribution margin per order), and the decision (OODA).
- Find the bottleneck in one place first, confirm candidate causes with numbers, and then interpret them with 7C.
- 7C is a common language for comparing different channels with the same seven questions.
- Divide channels into types by customer behavior, not by name, and allocate roles rather than ranking channels against each other.
- The criterion for comparing channels is contribution margin per order, not revenue, and the calculation procedure is continued in the *Price Is Strategy* handbook.
- Leave the decision as one sentence containing the main channel, supporting channels, channels to give up, the basis, and the execution tasks and KPIs.
- A channel is a chain of businesses that connects information, ownership, logistics, and payment, and channel strategy is not about the number of channels listed on but about designing a different reason to buy for each channel and deciding where to concentrate resources and what to give up.
- The decision has one main channel, one supporting channel, and one channel to give up, and the reason for the choice includes at least two of customer, bottleneck, 7C, and profit.
